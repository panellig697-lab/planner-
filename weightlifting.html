The Basics

Weightlifting is the practice of using external resistance — barbells, dumbbells, kettlebells, machines — to build strength, muscle, and work capacity. Two terms get used loosely and are worth separating:

Olympic Weightlifting

The competitive sport built around two lifts: the snatch and the clean & jerk. Judged on maximum weight lifted overhead or to the shoulders in a single explosive movement.

Weight Training

The broader practice of lifting weights for strength, hypertrophy (muscle growth), or general fitness — includes squats, deadlifts, presses, and accessory work. This is what most gym-goers mean by "lifting weights."

This guide focuses mostly on weight training, since that's what the vast majority of lifters actually do.

Programming

Random workouts produce random results. Progress comes from applying progressive overload — gradually increasing weight, reps, or volume over time — inside a structure you can actually recover from.

Beginner

Full-body, 3x/week. Linear progression: add weight every session on the main lifts. Simple beats clever here.

Intermediate

Upper/lower or push/pull/legs split, 4–5x/week. Weekly rather than session-to-session progression.

Advanced

Periodized blocks (volume → intensity → peak), often body-part splits. Progress is slow and must be tracked carefully.

Technique Cues

  • Squat: brace your core before descending, knees track over toes, hips and shoulders rise together
  • Deadlift: bar stays close to the shins/legs throughout, hinge at the hips, chest up, don't round the lower back
  • Bench: shoulder blades pulled back and down, feet planted, bar path travels in a slight arc, not straight up and down
  • Overhead Press: squeeze glutes to avoid over-arching, bar path close to the face, lock out fully overhead

Recovery & Nutrition

Lifting breaks muscle down; recovery is where it rebuilds stronger. Three levers matter most:

Sleep

7–9 hours. This is when most muscle repair and hormonal recovery happens — no supplement compensates for chronic sleep debt.

Protein

Roughly 1.6–2.2g per kg of bodyweight per day, spread across meals, supports muscle repair and growth.

Deload

Every 4–8 weeks, drop volume or intensity for a week to let joints and the nervous system recover before it becomes an injury.

Safety

Before you load up: learn the movement pattern with an empty bar or light weight first, use a spotter or safety pins for bench/squat, and never sacrifice form to hit a number. Pain that's sharp or joint-specific is a signal to stop, not push through — soreness is normal, pain is not.
Train consistently. Progress slowly. Recover deliberately.
